An essay on learning, work, and worth

The Age of Enough

A 7-minute read · Also on Medium ↗

We spent two centuries learning to earn a living. Now we get to learn how to live.

01 · The school we inherited

The classroom most of us sat in was designed for the factory. Bells marked the shifts. Desks stood in rows. Children moved through grades in batches, like parts along a line, and were graded on how well they produced the expected answer on time.

It was not a cruel design. It was a practical one. For most of history, the central problem was survival: food, shelter, a steady wage. A system that turned out reliable workers solved that problem for millions of families, including mine.

02 · Survival was the curriculum

Look closely and the whole system rewards one thing: being useful to someone else. Grades told you whether a teacher approved. Degrees told an employer you could be trusted. Titles and salaries told everyone else how far you had climbed.

Each of those was a proxy for the same question: will this person be able to earn a living? When survival was uncertain, that was the right question to ask. We built our self-worth on the answer.

03 · The math has changed

I have spent twenty years building data systems, and I have watched the cost of producing things fall, and then fall again. First machines took over physical work. Now software, and AI in particular, is taking over a large share of the thinking work that used to need a room full of people.

We are not fully there yet, and the gains are not evenly shared. But the direction is clear. We are moving toward a world where survival is not the hard problem it once was. A world of abundance.

04 · When survival is solved, what is left to prove?

If a machine can produce the answer, being the person who produces the answer stops being impressive. The old scoreboards (the grade, the title, the number on the offer letter) lose their meaning, because they were keeping score of a game we no longer have to play.

That can feel like a loss. I think it is a release. When there is nothing left to prove to the world, the only person left to prove anything to is yourself.

When there is nothing left to prove to the world, the only person left to prove anything to is yourself.

05 · The only audience that counts

Proving your worth to yourself is harder than it sounds. There is no grade, no promotion, no applause. You have to decide what you stand for, hold yourself to it, and notice honestly when you fall short.

I learned this the expensive way. Years ago I started a food business in India. By every outside measure, it did not work out. What I kept was more useful than the money: I knew I had built something real, I knew exactly where I had been wrong, and I did not need anyone to tell me either.

06 · Peace of mind is the new reward

If external validation was the reward system of scarcity, internal peace is the reward system of abundance. Not the absence of ambition, but ambition pointed inward: to be curious, to build well, to be honest with yourself, and to be content with the person doing the work.

That changes what learning should be for. Less memorizing, more asking. Less competing for a ranking, more building things that matter to you. Less fear of being wrong, more practice at noticing it. The goal is not to be employable. It is to be whole.

07 · Solve it once, for everyone

Every hard problem I have worked on had already been solved somewhere, by someone, and then solved again and again in rooms that never talked to each other. Much of the effort in any organization goes into rediscovering what someone else already knew.

In a world of abundance, that repeated effort is the real scarcity. If every person could take on a problem, solve it, and share the solution freely, no one would spend years rebuilding the same answer. Their energy would go to the next problem instead.

That is how a civilization compounds. Scientists describe a Type I civilization as one that can harness all the energy available on its home planet. We will not get there by working harder on problems already solved. We get there when every solution becomes everyone's starting point.

This is where the two halves of this essay meet. When you no longer need applause, giving your best work away costs you nothing.

What I believe

  1. Education should teach people how to live, not only how to earn.
  2. Self-worth that depends on someone else's approval can be taken away. Self-worth you earn from yourself cannot.
  3. Abundance is a responsibility. The systems that create it have to be trustworthy.
  4. Being wrong, and noticing it, is the fastest way to learn anything.
  5. Peace of mind is not the prize at the end. It is the way you walk.
  6. Solve a problem once, then give the answer away.
